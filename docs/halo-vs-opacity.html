Static halo vs opacity only

Both modes fade a strike out over 60 minutes using exactly the same opacity ramp, and both play exactly the same arrival animation. The only difference is what happens after the animation finishes, for strikes still under 5 minutes old.

1. The same strike at nine ages

This is the crux. Read the two rows against the age labels underneath. The opacity ramp holds at 100% for the whole 0–5 minute band, then eases down — so in opacity-only mode a strike that landed 10 seconds ago and one that landed 5 minutes ago are pixel-identical, and a 7-minute-old one is only 4% dimmer. Recency is not readable. The halo is what makes that band legible.

What you are trading

  • Static halo — a strike you never saw arrive is still identifiable as recent for 5 minutes. Costs a permanent visual difference on every young marker, which at storm density is a lot of glowing at once.
  • Opacity only — quieter and cleaner, and the animation is the only thing competing for attention. But recency exists only in the moment it happens: miss the animation and the information is gone until the strike is old enough for the opacity ramp to separate it.

Which is right depends on whether someone is watching the map continuously or glancing at it. For a glanceable dashboard the halo earns its keep; for a map someone is actively watching play back, opacity-only is less noisy.
